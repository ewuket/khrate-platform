# 02 — Group-Buying: Global Research & Lessons for KHRATE

This synthesises how collective/community/social commerce has played out across
markets, and extracts only the lessons that transfer to Kigali. It is deliberately
opinionated: the goal is to avoid repeating expensive, well-documented mistakes.

> Note on sourcing: this is a synthesis of well-established, widely reported industry
> patterns (2018–2025). Where specific numbers matter for a founder decision they are
> flagged as **assumptions to validate**, not facts to build on blindly.

## The models, and what actually happened

### China — "Community Group Buying" (社区团购): Pinduoduo, Meituan Select, Xingsheng Youxuan, Didi Chengxin
The canonical model. A **团长 (tuánzhǎng / group leader)** — usually someone who runs a
small neighbourhood shop or is a trusted local resident — collects orders from a
WeChat group by a nightly cut-off. The platform aggregates all orders in an area,
buys/procures the next morning, and delivers **in bulk to the leader's pickup point**
the same or next day. Customers collect from the leader. Leader earns ~10% commission.

- **Why it worked:** next-day, pre-sold, pickup-based logistics = almost zero last-mile
  cost and almost zero waste (you only buy what's already sold). Trust rode on an
  existing local relationship. Fresh produce economics finally worked online.
- **Why it burned:** 2020–2021 the giants subsidised prices below cost to grab share.
  Regulators intervened ("nine no's"). Several shut down (Didi, Alibaba's Freshippo
  arm retrenched). **Lesson: the logistics model is brilliant; the subsidy war is a
  trap. Do not buy growth by selling below cost.**

### Africa
- **Nigeria — Sabi, Suplias, Omnibiz, TradeDepot:** these are mostly **B2B** — aggregating
  demand from small *retailers* (kiosks/dukas), not consumers. Profitable direction:
  informal retail is huge and fragmented. **Lesson: B2B micro-retail aggregation is a
  real adjacent market KHRATE could grow into.**
- **Kenya — Twiga Foods:** aggregated farmer supply → sold to vendors. Struggled on
  unit economics and cash burn, heavy layoffs 2023–24. **Lesson: owning too much of the
  supply chain (sourcing + logistics + credit) too early is fatal. Stay asset-light.**
- **South Africa — stokvels:** informal rotating savings/bulk-buying clubs, deeply
  cultural, often used for bulk grocery buying at year-end. **Lesson: collective buying
  is already a trusted social behaviour in Africa. KHRATE is formalising something
  people already do, not importing a foreign idea.**

### Southeast Asia
- **Indonesia — Super, KitaBeli, Bibit:** replicated the China leader model in second/third-tier
  towns. Super reached profitability by focusing on **staples with long shelf life** (rice,
  oil, sugar, packaged goods) rather than fragile fresh produce, and by keeping delivery to
  agent pickup points. **Lesson: launch on shelf-stable staples; add fresh carefully.**

### India — Meesho, DealShare, Facebook/WhatsApp group buying
- **DealShare** grew on WhatsApp-native group deals in non-metro India, then struggled as
  discounts dried up. **Meesho** succeeded as social reselling. **Lesson: WhatsApp is the
  distribution channel, not a feature. In Rwanda WhatsApp penetration is very high — the
  invite/share loop must be WhatsApp-first.**

### Latin America — Facily (Brazil), Justo
- **Facily** grew explosively on social group buying then collapsed (2023) under fraud,
  logistics chaos, and unsustainable incentives. **Lesson: fraud and operational control
  are not back-office details — they kill these businesses. Build fraud controls and
  operational accountability from day one.**

### Europe / North America — Groupon, and grocery (Instacart, Ocado)
- **Groupon** = deal aggregation, not grocery; taught everyone that discount-chasing
  customers don't retain. **Instacart/Ocado** = expensive, on-demand, individual
  last-mile delivery. **Lesson: the rich-world on-demand model is exactly what KHRATE
  must NOT copy — its cost structure is impossible in Kigali.**

## What transfers to Kigali — the distilled rules

1. **Pre-sell, then buy.** Aggregate orders to a cut-off, procure against confirmed
   demand. This is the single most important lesson — it kills waste and working-capital
   risk on fresh produce.
2. **Pickup points / neighbourhood drop beats individual doorstep.** Delivery density is
   everything. One vehicle to one estate/office beats ten scattered drops.
3. **Ride existing trust.** Group leaders / community organisers who already have
   relationships (estate WhatsApp admins, office colleagues, church/co-op members).
4. **WhatsApp is the growth engine**, not an afterthought.
5. **Staples first, fresh carefully.** Shelf-stable goods de-risk the launch.
6. **Never subsidise below cost.** Value comes from real aggregation savings and lower
   logistics cost, not from burning investor money.
7. **Fraud & operational control from day one**, especially around leader payouts,
   Mobile Money proof, and "did the customer actually collect".
8. **Stay asset-light.** Don't own farms, trucks, or give supplier credit early.

## What we explicitly reject

- On-demand 30-minute delivery. Wrong cost structure for Kigali.
- Deep discounting / subsidy-funded growth. Facily and the China price war are the graves.
- Owning the whole supply chain early (Twiga's mistake).
- Fake urgency, fake reviews, fake popularity counters. The founder brief forbids this and
  the research shows discount-trust customers don't retain anyway.
